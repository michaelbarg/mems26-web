## 0. TL;DR — the single root problem

S2 does **not** lose because stops are too tight or sizing is wrong. It loses because of
**asymmetric trade management**: every losing trade pays the **full −1.0 R**, while winning
trades are capped at a tiny **+0.75 R average** (27 of 39 winners book only the T1 partial,
0.04–0.27 R, then the runner is stopped back at break-even).

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Win rate | 50.0 % (39 W / 38 L / 1 BE) | CSV `outcome` |
| Avg **win** | **+0.746 R** | CSV `pnl_r`, outcome=WIN |
| Avg **loss** | **−1.000 R** (all 38 losers are exactly −1R) | CSV `pnl_r`, outcome=LOSS |
| Expectancy | **−0.114 R / trade** | computed |
| Break-even avg-win needed at 50 % WR | **≥ 0.97 R** | `lossrate/winrate` |

At a 50 % win rate you must average **≥ 0.97 R** per winner just to break even. S2 delivers
0.75 R. **The asymmetry *is* the −$2,127.** Fix the winner side (let runners run on a
structure trail) and S2 flips positive without touching entries.

## 1. How S2 currently sets risk per pattern

### 1.1 Stop (entry → stop)
Computed at fire in `five_min_system.py:1084-1159` via `adaptive_stop.compute_stop` /
`compute_stop_v2` (`STOP_ANCHORS_V2=1` is ON):
- **Structural anchor** per pattern (Reactive/OFA: the entry bar low/high,
  `five_min_system.py:1100-1103`; chart patterns: `info["structural_anchor"]`).
- **ATR cap** = `today_typical × ATR_MULTIPLIERS[family]` (`adaptive_stop.py:33-39`,
  Reactive 1.0× / OFA 1.5× / Flag 1.5× / Double 2.0× / HnS 2.0×). Under V2 the structural
  stop *wins* and the cap becomes a **size** gate (`compute_stop_v2`, `cap_exceeded`).
- **Floor** = 4 ticks / 1.0 pt minimum, ATR-relative when `S2_ATR_RELATIVE=1`
  (`adaptive_stop.py:66-71`).

**Observed original stop distance (from the 38 −1R losers, whose stop never trailed):**

| Pattern | n (losers) | median stop (pts) | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| INITIATIVE_SHORT | 5 | 4.0 | 3.0 | 5.5 |
| REACTIVE_LONG | 12 | 8.5 | 4.0 | 17.75 |
| REACTIVE_SHORT | 16 | 8.5 | 4.5 | 21.75 |
| INITIATIVE_LONG | 1 | 13.0 | — | — |
| BULL_FLAG_LONG | 3 | 15.5 | 8.5 | 15.75 |
| VEGAS* | 1 | 22.5 | — | — |

Median 1R ≈ **8.5 pts ($42.50/contract)** for the dominant Reactive family. **Stops are not
too tight** — they are reasonable-to-wide. (The 27 trades that *look* like a 1-tick stop in
the raw `stop` column are winners whose stop was trailed to BE+1T after T1 — see §3, not an
entry-stop bug.)

### 1.2 Targets T1 / T2 / T3
- **Reactive / Initiative (OFA):** R-based off the day-type targets table
  (`five_min_system.py:1402-1431`). Measured true R:R **at fire** (from losers, stop=original):
  **T1 ≈ 0.75 R, T2 ≈ 2.0 R.** T3 is usually `None` (trail per day type).
- **H&S / Double:** pattern-measure based (0.50× / 0.66–0.74× depth, `:1308-1324`).
- **Flags:** T1 slides 0.8→0.4 R by stop width (`:1325-1353`); T2 = full pole or a
  VAH/VAL/POC reference by day type (`:1364-1398`).

### 1.3 Contract split & sizing
- Split per pattern (`contract_split.py:15-30`): OFA **25/50/25**, H&S & Double **33/33/34**,
  Flags **50/50/0**.
- Size (#contracts) from the Auth Table (`auth_table_v1.py`, pattern × day_type × quality
  tier → 0–3). V2 sizing additionally cuts contracts when `cap_exceeded`
  (`five_min_system.py:1169-1215`).
- **I-34 (sizing-computed-but-not-applied) — NOT reproduced in this dataset.** All 38 losers
  are exactly −1.00 R (`pnl_r.describe`: min=25%=50%=75%=max=−1.0), i.e. risk-per-trade is
  *uniform in R*. A sizing bug that fired full-size where reduced-size was due would show up
  as **R-magnitude** consistency but **$-magnitude** spread, which we do see (different stop
  widths × contracts) — but there is no evidence of mis-applied R. Sizing is working in R
  terms; **the leak is management, not sizing.** (The split itself is fine; it's the runner
  exit that fails — §3.)

> \* `VEGAS`/`ZLR` (3 rows) and 2 blank-pattern rows are Woodies/S4 patterns mislabeled
> `firing_system=2` in older data. They are immaterial to the conclusion (net of all 5 ≈ −$268)
> and are excluded from per-pattern S2 reasoning where noted.

## 2. Where the risk is mismanaged — with numbers

### 2.1 Winners are amputated; losers run full (the core failure)
- **All 39 winners hit T1. Only 5 hit T2. ZERO hit T3.** (CSV `*_hit_ts`.)
- After T1 the stop snaps to **BE+1T** (`manager.py:282-314`, `_apply_smart_be_after_t1`).
  **27 of 39 winners then exit at the entry price** (|exit−entry| ≤ 0.5 pt) for 0.04–0.27 R —
  the T1 slice (25 % of size) *is the entire profit*; the 75 % runner earns ~0.
- Result: avg winner **+0.746 R**, avg loser **−1.000 R**, expectancy **−0.114 R**.

**Expectancy sensitivity (win rate held at 50 %, loss at −1R):**

| If avg win = | Expectancy |
|---|---|
| 0.75 R (today) | **−0.125 R** |
| 1.00 R | 0.00 R |
| 1.25 R | +0.125 R |
| **1.50 R** | **+0.25 R** |
| 2.00 R | +0.50 R |

Lifting the average winner from 0.75 R to **1.5 R flips S2 from −0.125 to +0.25 R/trade** —
roughly **−$2,127 → +$1,300** on the same 78 entries, purely from runner management.

### 2.2 T1 is below 1R
True T1 ≈ **0.75 R** for Reactive/Initiative. Even a "winning" trade that only fills T1 books
**less than the 1 R it risked**. Combined with BE-after-T1, winners *cannot structurally*
average ≥ 1 R. This is the mechanical reason §2.1 holds.

### 2.3 REACTIVE is the whole loss
REACTIVE_LONG + REACTIVE_SHORT = 50 trades, **net −$2,294.92** ≈ the entire S2 loss
(everything else nets roughly flat-to-positive). Reactive winners avg **+0.46 R**, losers
**−1.0 R**, win rate **44 %** → that pattern needs avg win **≥ 1.27 R** to break even and gets
0.46 R.

| Pattern | n | net $ | W | L | avg R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| REACTIVE_LONG | 18 | −1296 | 6 | 12 | −0.56 |
| REACTIVE_SHORT | 32 | −999 | 16 | 16 | −0.24 |
| BULL_FLAG_LONG | 3 | −596 | 0 | 3 | −1.00 |
| INITIATIVE_LONG | 4 | +56 | 3 | 1 | +1.36 |
| INITIATIVE_SHORT | 14 | +175 | 9 | 5 | +0.13 |
| BEAR_FLAG_SHORT | 2 | +504 | 2 | 0 | +1.60 |

Note the contrast: **INITIATIVE_LONG (+1.36 R) and BEAR_FLAG_SHORT (+1.60 R)** — the few
trades that *were allowed to run* (hit T2) — are exactly the profitable ones. The data already
shows that runners pay; the system just rarely lets them.

### 2.4 The structural trail that *should* run is dead code
- The real structure trail — **`TrailEngine`** (`services/trail_engine.py`: Layer-1 HL/LH
  5-bar swing trail, Layer-2 chandelier, Layer-3 time stop, Layer-4 context) — is
  **never instantiated in production.** `grep "TrailEngine(" backend/ --include=*.py` returns
  **only tests**; `backend/main.py:735` wires **`BarLevelDetector`**, not `TrailEngine`.
- Worse, even if it *were* wired, its swing/chandelier layers only arm **after T2**
  (`trail_engine.py:219` `if trade.t2_hit_ts is None: return`). Since only 5/39 S2 winners
  reach T2, the structural trail would still essentially never run.
- So the **only** active runner logic is `BarLevelDetector.apply_trail_after_t1`
  (`bar_level_detector.py:99-107` + `manager.py:339-432`, `RUNNER_TRAIL_V1=1`): a chandelier
  `hwm − 1×initial_risk` with a **BE+1T floor**, engaged right after T1. On the first pullback
  it collapses to BE+1T → the 27 scratches. **It is not a structural trail and it gives the
  runner no room.**

## 3. Why "98.7 % of exits are STOP_HIT" is a measurement artifact (read before acting)
The raw `exit_reason` breakdown is **77 STOP_HIT / 1 manual** — but this does **not** mean 77
full-stop losses. `exit_reason` records the **final** exit mechanism. After T1, the stop is
trailed to BE+1T, so a *winner* that pulls back also exits via "STOP_HIT" (the BE stop) with
`outcome=WIN`. Correct decomposition:

- **38 genuine −1R losses** (stop never moved; `hit_t1=False`; pnl_r=−1.0).
- **39 wins, all via the trailed stop** — **27** of them are sub-0.3 R BE-scratches.

So the real story is not "stops are firing too early on good trades"; it's "**winners get
flattened to BE the instant they take the T1 partial, so they never become 1.5–3 R trades**,
while losers always go the full distance." That is the asymmetry to fix.

## 4. Recommendations (concrete, S2-specific)

**R1 — Wire and use a structural runner trail; stop flattening winners to BE.** (highest impact)
The dynamic structure trail (§5) is the fix. Either wire the existing `TrailEngine` into
`main.py` *and* drop its T2 gate to a **T1/C1 gate** (`trail_engine.py:219`), or replace
`apply_trail_after_t1`'s chandelier with a swing-structure trail. Give the runner room: trail
to the **prior swing point**, not BE+1T, after the first profit.

**R2 — Raise T1 to ≥ 1.0 R (or shrink the T1 slice).** T1 at 0.75 R guarantees sub-1R winners
(`five_min_system.py:1402-1431`). Options, YAML-tunable (per the standing
config-tunability decision): set T1 ≥ 1.0 R, or cut the T1 contract from 25 %→20 % and let the
runner carry more (`contract_split.py`). Backtest both — note `T1_LADDER_V2` (widen-T1) was
already **REJECTED** by backtest (FLAG_INDEX: flat −$144 / struct −$624), so widening must be
validated, not assumed.

**R3 — Make the risk circuit-breakers apply in SHADOW too (observability), and fix the
"scratch resets the counter" bug.** The 2-stop cooldown and SSV should treat a **BE scratch as
non-progress**, not as a WIN that resets the streak (`cooldown.py:25-34` keys off `"STOP"`
only; `_set_outcome` marks +0.04 R as WIN). Consider counting "stopped at ≤ BE after entry" as
a cooldown event. Separately, surface the daily-loss / max-trades / consecutive-loss caps in
SHADOW as **shadow-blocks** (log-only) so we can measure their effect before LIVE.

**R4 — Reactive needs the most runner help (or a tighter entry filter).** REACTIVE = −$2,295
of −$2,127. With avg win 0.46 R it needs ≥ 1.27 R to break even — only a real runner trail
gets it there. If R1 doesn't lift Reactive's avg win materially in backtest, gate Reactive
harder (it is already `S2 ⟂ S3`, firing on geometry+volume alone).

**R5 — Don't chase tighter stops.** Median 1R is 8.5 pts; tightening would raise the −1R count.
The lever is the **numerator** (let winners run), not the denominator.

## 5. How Michael's new dynamic management rule changes S2

**The rule (DEFAULT mgmt; Woodies/S4 keeps its own settings):**
> C1 takes a first profit target → stop to **break-even**. Remaining contracts trail by
> **STRUCTURE**: the moment price makes a **new structural place**, move the stop to it and set
> the next target at the **EARLIER of {that new place, the next important line
> (POC/VAH/VAL/IB/PDH/PDL)}**; repeat through T3 while the move continues. **One trade at a
> time.**

### 5.1 What it changes vs the current fixed T1/T2/T3
| Today (fixed ladder) | New (dynamic structure trail) |
|---|---|
| C1 hits T1 (0.75 R) → **whole stop to BE+1T**, runner dies on first pullback (27/39 scratches) | C1 hits first target → stop to BE; runner **trails the most recent swing**, so a pullback that holds structure keeps the runner alive |
| T2 = fixed 2.0 R; reached 5/39 times; T3 fixed/None, reached 0 times | Next target = nearest structure **or** the next real line (POC/VAH/VAL/IB/PDH/PDL); ratchets forward only while the trend persists |
| Winner ceiling ≈ T1 slice ≈ 0.75 R avg | Winner can compound to 1.5–3 R **when the move continues**, while still locked at ≥ BE once C1 fires |
| `TrailEngine` (structure) dead; `apply_trail_after_t1` chandelier+BE floor is all that runs | Requires the structure trail to actually run from **C1/T1**, not T2 — exactly what is missing |

### 5.2 Would it have helped the S2 losers?
Two distinct cohorts:

- **The 38 −1R losers (`hit_t1=False`):** they **never reached the first target**, so the new
  rule's trail never engages — they would still be **−1 R**. *The dynamic rule does not reduce
  the loss side.* (That is fine — losers *should* take the full, reasonable 1R; tightening them
  is R5's anti-recommendation.)
- **The 39 winners (all hit T1), esp. the 27 BE-scratches:** this is where the rule pays.
  Today the runner is BE-capped and dies; under a structure trail it stays in as long as price
  keeps making higher-lows (LONG) / lower-highs (SHORT). The two patterns that *already* ran to
  T2 — INITIATIVE_LONG **+1.36 R** and BEAR_FLAG_SHORT **+1.60 R** — are the empirical proof
  that S2 runners reach 1.5 R+ **when allowed**.

### 5.3 Quantified estimate
The new rule changes **avg win**, not win rate or loss size. Using the §2.1 sensitivity:
- If the structure trail lifts avg win from **0.75 R → 1.5 R** (consistent with the two
  unflattened patterns), expectancy goes **−0.125 R → +0.25 R/trade**.
- Applied to these 78 entries: roughly **−$2,127 → ≈ +$1,300** (order-of-magnitude; exact value
  depends on per-trade MFE, which is not in `v9_trades` and should be measured by replaying bars
  past each T1 — `mfe_high`/`mfe_low` plumbing already exists in `trail_engine._update_mfe` but
  is dead because the engine is unwired).

**Bottom line:** the dynamic structure-trail attacks the exact half of the P&L that is broken
(the winner ceiling) and leaves the already-correct loser side (−1R) alone. It is the right
fix — **but it is currently impossible to execute because the structural `TrailEngine` is not
wired into `main.py` and what *is* wired flattens the runner to BE after T1.** Implementing the
rule = (a) run a structure trail from **C1/T1** (not T2), and (b) source "next important line"
from the live TPO/level data (`_load_sierra_tpo`, S/R levels already available in
`sr_proximity.py`). Validate in SHADOW + backtest before LIVE (Pre-LIVE discipline; and recall
`T1_LADDER_V2` was rejected on backtest, so prove this one with raw numbers).
