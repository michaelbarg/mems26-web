# ASYMMETRY — 17 live sessions, 2026-08-10 → 2026-09-07

Measured 2026-09-08 (cowork, read-only). 68 live trades / 17 sessions. MES = **$5/point**;
every $ figure below is `points × contracts × $5`.

## 0. Basis — and what "−$50.00" actually is

The −$50.00 headline is reproducible, and it is a **mixed basis**:

```sql
SELECT round(sum(coalesce(pnl_sierra, pnl_usd))::numeric,2)
  FROM v9_trades WHERE mode='live' AND entry_ts >= '2026-08-10';   -- => -50.00
```
`sum(pnl_usd)` alone = **+103.75**; `sum(pnl_sierra)` alone (33 non-null rows) = **−310.00**.
COALESCE takes the broker where it exists and the inflated book where it does not, so 35 of 68
rows enter at book value. Its per-day ranking is exactly the BEST/WORST list in the brief
(08-28 +348.75 · 09-02 +270.00 · 08-27 +123.75 · 09-01 −236.25 · 08-31 −203.75 · 08-14 −167.50)
— i.e. **three of those six days are book numbers, not broker numbers.**

Broker truth used here, in priority order (per trade):
1. **Sierra `Closed Trade Profit/Loss` records attributed to OUR order ids only** — parsed from
   `~/SierraChart/TradeActivityLogs/TradeActivityLog_<d>_UTC.37138283.data` with
   `scripts/sierra_activity_join.py:parse_activity`, then (a) the `Teton CME Routing historical
   balance` mirror records dropped (they duplicate 1–7 records/day; on 09-07 they doubled $2.50
   into $5.00), and (b) each record attached to the nearest fill ≤5 s earlier **whose order id is
   one of that trade's `quality.c1..c6_target/stop_id`**. Account 37138283 is shared with Eti, so
   the day aggregate is unusable: 5–28 Closed-P/L records per day belong to no order of ours.
   → 51 trades priced.
2. DLL fill journal `~/SierraChart_Data/v9_export/trade_fills_journal.jsonl`, where every
   contract is accounted for (`Σ leg contracts == ENTRY.contracts`) → +1.
3. `pnl_sierra` column → +2. **14 trades remain UNPRICED** (`MAE_SCRATCH` / `SIERRA_FLAT` /
   `phantom_reconcile` exits that never produced a matchable order id — the T-160 class).

**Validation:** this method returns **09-04 = −$116.25**, the exact figure T-256 verified
independently (`sierra_activity_join.py --date 2026-09-04`). Priced total over 17 sessions =
**+$523.75 / 54 trades**. Sign check: all 39 shorts, every all-STOP short reconstructs negative
(`points = entry − exit`); zero all-STOP trades show a positive P&L except break-even stops.

## 1. Size — were the winners smaller than the losers? No. **The losers were bigger.**

Contract count **is persisted**: `v9_trades.quality->>'contracts'`. It matches the DLL's own
`ENTRY.contracts` in `trade_fills_journal.jsonl` on **67 of 68** rows (the 68th, #831, has no
journal ENTRY). No reconstruction needed.

Stop distance is `|entry_price − quality.metadata.stop_initial|` — present on **68/68**. Do **not**
use the `stop` column: `SMART_BE` moved it 30 times, which drags the median winner's "stop
distance" to 0.25 pt and inverts the whole table.

| group | n | avg contracts | median ct | avg initial stop | median stop | sum $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WIN**  | 33 | **3.06** | 2 | **7.36 pt** | 6.88 | +2,337.50 |
| **LOSS** | 21 | **3.24** | 2 | **6.07 pt** | 5.75 | −1,813.75 |
| unpriced | 14 | 2.14 | — | 8.87 pt | — | n/a |

The sizer is a budget sizer (`RISK_BUDGET_SIZING_V1=1`, `RISK_BUDGET_USD=225`, ceiling
`FIXED_CONTRACTS_5=1`; before 01.09 the same slope came from `SIZE_CAP_CUT_V1`). It gives **more**
contracts to **narrower** stops — and the narrow-stop band is where the money is lost:

| initial risk | n | avg ct | W | L | net $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 4 pt | 2 | 3.00 | 2 | 0 | +13.75 |
| 4–5 | 5 | 3.00 | 1 | 4 | −191.25 |
| 5–6 | 13 | **3.69** | 6 | 7 | **−230.00** |
| 6–7 | 13 | 3.23 | 9 | 4 | +201.25 |
| 7–8 | 12 | 2.92 | 7 | 5 | +86.25 |
| 8–11 | 6 | 2.67 | 5 | 1 | +311.25 |
| ≥ 11 | 3 | 2.33 | 3 | 0 | +332.50 |

Contracts fall monotonically as risk rises; P&L rises monotonically as risk rises. **The sizer's
slope runs opposite to the edge.** By contract count: ct=5 → n=13, −$287.50 (the only losing size
class); ct=2 → n=27, +$345.00; ct=3 → n=3, +$383.75; ct=4 → n=10, +$30.00.

Robustness — the ordering holds under all three independent P&L bases:
broker (n=51): WIN ct 3.13 / risk 7.54 vs LOSS ct 3.30 / risk 5.98 ·
DLL-journal-complete (n=36): 3.18 / 7.60 vs 3.26 / 6.20 ·
`pnl_sierra` (n=33): 2.65 / 7.52 vs 2.94 / 6.34.

## 3. The single highest-ratio change

**Cap the order at 2 contracts whenever the initial stop is under 6.0 points.**

* **n = 12** trades where the cap binds (`risk_pts < 6` AND `contracts > 2`) — 8 losers, 4 winners,
  spanning 08-10 → 09-04 (5 of them predate `RISK_BUDGET_SIZING_V1`, so this is not one flag's bug).
* **Saved $467.50 · blocked $107.50 · ratio 4.35 · net +$360.00** over 17 sessions — against a
  priced book of +$523.75.
* Same sign and same direction under every basis: broker 4.35 (n=12) · DLL-journal 11.43 (n=9) ·
  `pnl_sierra` 307 (n=5).
* It is a pure linear scaling of an already-taken trade, so it can never turn a winner into a
  loser or change any entry decision — it only shrinks both sides of the 5–6 pt band.

Rejected alternatives, with their numbers: *skip `risk_pts < 6` entirely* → n=20, saved $936.25,
blocked $528.75, **ratio 1.77** (worse — it forfeits real winners). *Cap 2 when `risk_pts < 5`* →
ratio 18.22 but **n=3, fails n≥10**. *Flat 2 contracts always* → n=26, ratio 1.05, net +$29.58 —
statistically indistinguishable from doing nothing. *Any daily-loss-halt tightening* → n=0 until
−$150, where it is net-negative. *Any gate parameter change* → the four gates with ratio > 1 and
n ≥ 10 are already ON; the three biggest gates by volume (`awaiting_release`,
`entry_location_quality`, `daytype_playbook`, combined n=257) all block more money than they save
at the ceiling, so the change they suggest is *loosening*, which is the opposite of the goal and
which this data cannot license (first-blocker trap).

**Caveats that would change the answer.** n=12 clears the bar by two. 14 of 68 live trades are
unpriced and they carry the *widest* average stop (8.87 pt) — if they resolve as winners the
gradient steepens and the case strengthens; if they resolve as losers it weakens. 8 of 17 sessions
have no `mfe_track`, so §2 is a 9-session read. The DLL journal prices STOP legs at the *requested*
stop, not the fill (T-240), which is why the DLL and broker bases differ by $2.50–$25 per row.
