## 1. Executive Summary

The Quality Score anti-correlation is **NOT uniform across day types**. It is concentrated in **NORMAL** days, where 70+ score setups lose $-2,482 (WR 36.1%). Meanwhile, **DEVELOPING** and **RANGE_DAY** show normal (non-inverted) score-performance correlation, with 70+ buckets outperforming lower buckets. TREND_DAY sample is too small (n=3) for conclusions. **This is the Phase 3.3 lever**: the score works on some day types but is actively harmful on NORMAL days, which represent 52% of all setups.

## 8. Implication for Phase 3.3

The anti-correlation is not a fundamental scoring failure — it is a **day-type-specific miscalibration**. On NORMAL days, the current Quality Score weights (Vegas 30, TPO 25, FVG 25, Footprint 20) produce high scores for setups that systematically lose. This likely means the conditions that score well on NORMAL days (e.g., Vegas alignment, FVG presence) are actually fade signals on range-bound normal days, where mean-reversion dominates over momentum.

**Phase 3.3 lever:** Quality Score v2 should either (a) apply NORMAL-day-specific weight adjustments that invert or heavily discount Vegas/FVG alignment, or (b) use a separate scoring model for NORMAL days that captures mean-reversion signals instead of momentum signals. The existing weights work for DEVELOPING and RANGE_DAY — don't change those.
