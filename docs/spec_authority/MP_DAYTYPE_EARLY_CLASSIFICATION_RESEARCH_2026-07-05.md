# Market Profile Day-Type Early Classification: 30-Minute Read vs. End-of-Day Truth in S&P 500 Futures

## TL;DR
- A 30-minute read is a **directional bias hypothesis, not a day-type classification**: canonically the Initial Balance is the **first hour (two 30-min TPO periods, A+B)**, so one 30-min bar is at most a *half-IB proxy*. Dalton's own standard is that "the first half hour of trade establishes one of the day's extremes in the large majority of cases" — but he immediately adds it is "of little value unless a trader can identify which extreme will hold," which the first 30 minutes usually cannot.
- The single most decision-relevant early variable is **IB width relative to ATR**, and it only finalizes at 10:30 ET. On 5,519 ES/NQ days (2015–2025, tradingstats.net), narrow IBs (<0.5× ATR) broke 98.7% of the time with a 74.8% median extension; extreme IBs (>1.5× ATR) broke only 66.7% with the median extension collapsing to 22.3%. Most "trend day vs. range day" calls therefore cannot be made with confidence at the 30-minute mark.
- Treat the 30-minute read as a **hypothesis with roughly coin-flip-to-60% reliability**; the genuine confirmation gates come later — the **C-period (10:30–11:00)** close and **post-breakout retracement depth**. C-period confirmation more than doubles the full-extension rate (to 45.5% on ES); a shallow (<25%) retracement closes in the breakout direction 93.8% of the time.

## Key Findings

**1. Canonical definitions vs. practitioner shortcuts.** In canonical Market Profile (Steidlmayer's CBOT manuals; Dalton's *Mind Over Markets*), the Initial Balance is explicitly the **first hour** — the A and B periods (9:30–10:30 ET for US index futures). A "30-minute preliminary read" is not a canonical object; it uses only the A period. Practitioners (TRADEPRO, Noesis, FTMO, edgeful) freely adapt the IB window, and the "Opening Range" (first 30 min) is a distinct, older concept (Crabel's NR4/NR7 work, Fisher's ACD method) frequently conflated with the IB.

**2. Day type is, by canon, a retrospective classification.** Dalton/Steidlmayer day types (Normal, Normal Variation, Trend, Double-Distribution Trend, Neutral, Non-trend) are *defined by full-session structure* — total range vs. IB, range extension, and where the close sits. None can be definitively confirmed at 30 minutes; several (Neutral, Double-Distribution, Neutral-Extreme) only resolve in the afternoon. The Wikipedia/HandWiki survey of the CBOT manuals is blunt: day-type reading is "chart reading and forecasting" subject to "the multitude of potential interpretations for most any chart," and mastery "can well take 10,000 hours of training."

**3. The Dalton 30-minute claim, precisely.** *Mind Over Markets* (Ch. 4, "The Open," ~p. 62): "Seasoned traders... know that the first half hour of trade establishes one of the day's extremes in the large majority of cases. As accurate as this fact may be, it is of little value unless a trader can identify which extreme will hold throughout the day." Two things follow: (a) the claim is about the **first half hour (the A period)**, deliberately distinct from the full-hour IB Dalton defines elsewhere; (b) Dalton attaches **no percentage** — he says "large majority." The widely-circulated "50% of the time" rendering is a third-party paraphrase: it comes from PureAlgo's *Mind Over Markets* study notes (purealgocapital.com), which read verbatim "1st 30m of the day establishes 1 of the extremes 50% of the time." That "50%" appears to conflate Dalton's general "large majority" statement with his separate, narrower point that on an **Open-Rejection-Reverse** open the initial extreme "generally hold[s] less than half of the time." So: *that an early extreme is set* is high-probability; *which extreme, and whether it holds* is not resolved at 30 minutes.

**4. Opening type is the best 30-minute tell — but it keys off the open, not the clock.** Dalton's four open types (Open-Drive, Open-Test-Drive, Open-Rejection-Reverse, Open-Auction) are diagnosable in the first minutes and carry the strongest early signal: an **Open-Drive** "in the majority of cases" leaves an extreme that holds all day and frequently produces a Trend day, whereas an **Open-Auction** signals a rotational/balanced day. Higher-conviction opens → directional/trend days; lower-conviction opens → rotational days. This is practitioner-corroborated (Ratul Bhattacharya/thenatureofmarkets, marketcalls) but explicitly framed as probability: "Bias are GOOD, but we should not look at them as FACTS but rather as PROBABILITIES."

**5. IB width predicts day type — the strongest quantitative relationship.** Canonically, Dalton's "base/lamp" metaphor: "If the initial balance is narrow, the odds are greater that the base will be upset and range extension will occur. Days that establish a wider base... [are] more likely to maintain the extremes for the day." This is confirmed on a large sample (tradingstats.net, 5,519 ES+NQ days, 2015–2025):
   - **Narrow IB (<0.5× ATR, ~66% of ES days):** 98.7% break, **74.8%** median extension, 33.6% double-breakout.
   - **Normal IB (0.5–1.0×):** 96.6% break, 50.7% median extension.
   - **Wide IB (1.0–1.5×):** 93.5% break, 39.6% median extension.
   - **Extreme IB (>1.5×):** breaks "only 66.7% of the time — and the median extension drops to 22.3%"; a third see *no* breakout (the IB *is* the day's range).
   Caveat: IB width is only known at 10:30 (full hour). At 30 minutes you have half the eventual IB, biasing a naive reader toward over-predicting breakouts.

**6. IB extension is the confirmation/revision engine.** Range extension beyond the IB signals OTF (other-timeframe) entry — this is what *upgrades* a tentative call to Trend/Normal-Variation or *revises* it:
   - ~97.8% of ES days break the IB at least once; **65.2%** of first breakouts occur in the **C-period (10:30–11:00)**, and >80% by 11:30.
   - **C-period confirmation:** "When the C-period confirms the breakout, 45.5% of ES days reach 100% extension upside. When it fails, 37.9% close back inside the IB." That 45.5% (and 50.0% on the downside) versus an unconditional ~18–20% means confirmation more than doubles the full-extension rate.
   - **Retracement depth is decisive:** shallow (<25%) retracement → close in breakout direction 93.8% (ES); deep (≥50%) → only 24.8% hold direction and 52.7% become *double-breakouts* (the early call flips). "About one-third of all first breakouts fail — price breaks the IB boundary at some point but closes back inside."
   - **IB formation sequence:** "the IB tends to break opposite to whichever extreme formed first" — if the high forms first, a single-down breakout is "roughly twice as likely" as single-up.

**7. Day-type frequencies (S&P-relevant).** Jan Firich's quantitative study (US equities, *Futures Trading Based on Market Profile Day Timeframe Structures*, 2012, IQFeed data) reports, Table 1 "DTS Occurrence" verbatim: **Non-trend 6.81%, Normal 2.43%, Normal Variation 41.77%, Trend 9.54%, Neutral 30.21%.** Firich found the IB is broken in "more than 81%" of all trading days (corroborated by a NinjaTrader six-month NQ report at 82.17% for single-direction breaks). Trend days are rare (~9.5%) but the most punishing to misclassify — that asymmetric cost is why early reads matter despite low base rates.

## Details

**Why a 30-minute read is structurally weaker than the canon assumes.** The canon uses a 60-minute IB precisely because the A period alone is noisy. Using only the first 30 minutes means: (i) you cannot yet compute IB width (the single strongest predictor); (ii) you cannot distinguish a Normal day (wide IB, holds) from the *start* of a Double-Distribution Trend day (narrow first hour, breaks later); (iii) Neutral days — where both IB extremes break — are by construction invisible at 30 minutes. The best 30-minute information is **open type** and **opening location vs. prior value/range** (in-value → rotational odds; out-of-range → directional odds), both of which Dalton frames as probabilities.

**Accuracy, stated honestly.** "An early extreme is set" is high-probability (Dalton's "large majority"); "the correct day type is locked in at 30 minutes" is low-probability. The genuine inflection where a preliminary read becomes reliable is the C-period and the first post-IB-break retracement — i.e., roughly 11:00–11:30 ET, a full hour *after* the 30-minute mark. An unconfirmed early directional guess sits around coin-flip-to-60% (consistent with Firich's note that even experienced traders find intraday day-type determination "difficult"), whereas C-period-confirmed plus shallow-retracement reads run 80–94%.

**Can the first 30-minute period serve as a partial IB proxy?** Yes, but explicitly as a *half-IB*. The A-period range is a usable provisional bracket and the open-type read is valid immediately, but every width-based probability above is calibrated to the **full hour**. A 30-minute "IB" is narrower than the true IB, biasing toward over-predicting breakouts. Practitioners who shorten the IB (common in 24-hour or non-US products) should re-derive their own width tiers rather than borrow the 60-minute statistics.

**How IB extension confirms or revises a day-type call.** A tentative "trend" lean (narrow IB + Open-Drive) is *confirmed* when the C-period closes beyond the IB and the subsequent retracement is shallow — one-timeframing (successive higher highs/higher lows without overlap) is the structural fingerprint. It is *revised* when the breakout fails back inside the IB (~34%) or the retracement is deep (≥50%, over half becoming Neutral/double-distribution). Thus the IB extension is not a one-time confirmation but a continuously updating signal through the C–E periods.

**Distinguishing canon from practitioner opinion.** *Canonical* (Steidlmayer manuals, Dalton books): IB = first hour; six day types defined by full-session structure; narrow-IB→breakout / wide-IB→contained; range extension signals OTF entry; the "first half hour establishes an extreme in the large majority of cases." *Practitioner/third-party*: specific percentages (Firich's frequencies; tradingstats' breakout/extension ladder; the "50%" paraphrase from PureAlgo; the "1.5–2× IB" target; "wait until 10:30 to trade"); shortened IB windows; mechanical IB-break entry rules. The percentages are useful but come from varied samples, instruments, and IB definitions and should not be treated as canon.

## Recommendations
- **Stage 1 (first 30 min / A period):** Form a *hypothesis only*. Read open type (Open-Drive/Test-Drive favor trend; Open-Auction favors rotation) and open location vs. prior value/range. Do not label the day; treat directional confidence as ≤60%.
- **Stage 2 (10:30 ET, IB complete):** Compute IB width ÷ 14-day ATR. Narrow (<0.5×) → lean breakout/trend; wide (>1.0×) → lean contained/range. This is the first point a day-type *lean* is statistically supported.
- **Stage 3 (C-period, 10:30–11:00):** Use the C-period close as the primary confirmation gate. C closes beyond IB in the break direction → upgrade toward Trend/Normal-Variation (full-extension odds ~45–50% vs. ~19% unconditional). C fails back inside → expect rotational/Neutral; ~38% close inside the IB.
- **Stage 4 (post-break retracement):** Shallow (<25% of IB) → hold the directional call (≈94% close in direction). Deep (≥50%) → *revise*; over half become double-breakout/Neutral days.
- **Benchmarks that flip the call:** No IB break by noon → ~1-in-5 (NQ) / 1-in-7 (ES) chance the day stays contained (favor Non-Trend/Normal). A failed first ES breakout reverses to the opposite side ~37% of the time. Recalibrate width tiers if you use any IB window other than 60 minutes.
- **Risk asymmetry:** Because Trend days are only ~9.5% but inflict the worst losses on faders, weight the cost of *missing* a trend above the cost of a false trend signal. When one-timeframing + Open-Drive + narrow IB + C-period confirmation align, stop fading — per Dalton (via OrderFlow Labs), "the worst thing a trader can do is not believe a trend day."

## Caveats
- **Sample/definition heterogeneity:** Firich (2012), tradingstats (2015–2025), and Noesis (~1,337 sessions) use different periods, instruments, and sometimes IB windows; percentages corroborate direction but are not strictly comparable. The "frequency table" (Normal 2.43%, Normal Variation 41.77%, Neutral 30.21%, Trend 9.54%, Non-Trend 6.81%) traces to Firich's IQFeed-based study and is widely re-circulated without each re-poster restating the methodology.
- **30-minute IB is non-canonical:** all 60-minute breakout/extension statistics mis-calibrate if applied to a 30-minute "IB."
- **Regime dependence:** Pit-era assumptions (Steidlmayer/Dalton) predate 24-hour electronic markets; overnight inventory now shapes the open. The IB-break rate has stayed remarkably stable (96.5–99.2% on ES, 2015–2025), but extension magnitude varies year to year (2018 and 2022 were high-extension outliers).
- **Inherent subjectivity:** the canon itself concedes day-type reading is interpretive forecasting; a single-day profile is "not a reliable measure of market condition" (a three-day minimum is preferred). Treat any single-session 30-minute classification as the weakest link.
- **Forward-looking language:** practitioner sources use "tends to," "favors," "expect" — probabilistic leanings, not deterministic outcomes, and presented as such here.

## סיכום בעברית

קריאה מוקדמת של "סוג היום" (day type) אחרי 30 דקות בחוזי S&P 500 (ES) היא **השערה כיוונית, לא סיווג ודאי**. לפי ההגדרה הקנונית (Steidlmayer, ו-Dalton ב-*Mind Over Markets*), ה-Initial Balance הוא **השעה הראשונה** (שני פרקי 30 דקות, A+B), ולכן 30 דקות הן רק "חצי IB". דלטון כותב שחצי השעה הראשונה "קובעת אחד מקצוות היום ברוב המכריע של המקרים", אך מדגיש מיד שזה "חסר ערך עד שיודעים *איזה* קצה יחזיק" — מה שבדרך כלל לא ניתן לדעת ב-30 דקות. (הנתון הנפוץ "50%" אינו של דלטון אלא פרפראזה מסיכומי PureAlgo.)

**רוחב ה-IB ביחס ל-ATR** הוא המנבא החזק ביותר, אך הוא נסגר רק ב-10:30. על 5,519 ימי ES/NQ (2015–2025): IB צר (<0.5× ATR) נשבר ב-98.7% עם הרחבה חציונית 74.8%; IB קיצוני (>1.5×) נשבר רק ב-66.7% וההרחבה החציונית צונחת ל-22.3%. אישורי המפתח מגיעים מאוחר יותר: **סגירת C-period** (10:30–11:00) מכפילה את הסיכוי להרחבה מלאה (ל-45.5% ב-ES), ו**עומק הנסיגה** אחרי הפריצה מכריע — נסיגה רדודה (<25%) → סגירה בכיוון הפריצה ב-93.8%, נסיגה עמוקה (≥50%) → רוב הימים הופכים ל-double-breakout/Neutral.

**שכיחות סוגי הימים** (Firich 2012): Normal Variation ~41.8%, Neutral ~30.2%, Trend ~9.5%, Non-Trend ~6.8%, Normal ~2.4%; ה-IB נשבר ביותר מ-81% מהימים.

**המלצה מעשית בשלבים:** (1) ב-30 דקות — רק השערה (סוג הפתיחה + מיקום ביחס לערך של אתמול), ביטחון ≤60%; (2) ב-10:30 — חישוב רוחב ה-IB; (3) ב-C-period — אישור או ביטול; (4) אחרי הפריצה — החלטה לפי עומק הנסיגה. ימי מגמה (Trend) נדירים (~9.5%) אך מסוכנים למתנגדים — כשכל הסימנים מתיישרים, אין להילחם במגמה. חשוב להבחין בין ההגדרות הקנוניות לבין דעות/סטטיסטיקות של סוחרים (אחוזים ספציפיים, חלונות IB מקוצרים) שאינן קנוניות.
